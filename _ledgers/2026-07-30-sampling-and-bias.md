## The article's thesis

`[EXAM]` Bias is a property of the centre, not the spread. Framework 1.12.A.1, quoted in the
article verbatim: bias is "a systematic error in the sampling procedure that results in a
statistic being consistently larger or consistently smaller than the parameter the statistic
is used to estimate."

`[STANDARD]` Increasing the sample size reduces variability and leaves bias untouched. This
follows from the definition above and is demonstrated rather than asserted.

## Vocabulary and methods

`[EXAM]` Simple random sample: "In a simple random sample (SRS) of size n, every sample of
the size n has the same chance of being selected." (1.11.A.3)

`[EXAM]` Stratified: division into "non-overlapping groups, called strata, based on one or
more shared attributes or characteristics (homogeneous grouping)", an SRS within each,
combined into one sample. (1.11.A.4)

`[EXAM]` Cluster: division into clusters where "ideally, each cluster mirrors the
heterogeneity of the population, with clusters similar to one another"; an SRS of clusters;
data from all units in the selected clusters. (1.11.A.5)

`[EXAM]` Systematic: "a random starting point and a fixed, periodic interval between
successive sampling units." (1.11.A.6)

`[STANDARD]` Stratified sampling reduces variability because strata are internally
homogeneous; cluster sampling is a logistical convenience because clusters are meant to
mirror the whole population. The framework states the homogeneity/heterogeneity contrast
(1.11.A.4 and 1.11.A.5) and says only that methods differ in appropriateness (1.11.B.1); the
variance-reduction consequence is standard sampling theory rather than a quoted claim.

`[EXAM]` Population, sample, parameter, statistic: used consistently with the framework and
with the existing article on writing parameters, which the prose links.

**Removed:** the term "sampling frame" does not appear in the essential-knowledge statements
for topics 1.10 to 1.13, and the article no longer uses it. See the corrections section at
the foot of this file.

## The named biases

`[EXAM]` All four, quoted or paraphrased from 1.12.A:

- Voluntary response bias — "may occur when a sample consists entirely of volunteers"
  (1.12.A.2)
- Undercoverage — "when the sampling method fails to include part of the population or a
  part of the population is less likely to be selected" (1.12.A.3)
- Nonresponse — "failure to obtain responses from some individuals chosen to be sampled.
  The respondents and nonrespondents could differ significantly" (1.12.A.4)
- Response bias — "responses … tend to differ from the 'true' value in one direction",
  with question wording bias and self-reporting named as examples (1.12.A.5)

`[EXAM]` Convenience and voluntary response are named together as nonrandom methods that
"introduce potential bias because they do not use random chance to select the individuals."
(1.12.A.6)

## The four-cell structure

Every cell is sourced.

`[EXAM]` Random selection licenses generalisation: "When observational units, or
experimental units, in a sample are randomly selected from a population, it is appropriate
to make generalizations about the entire population of individuals from which the sample was
selected." (1.10.E.2)

`[EXAM]` Without random selection, "it is appropriate to make generalizations only about a
population of individuals that are similar to those used in the study." (1.10.E.4)

`[EXAM]` Random assignment licenses causation: "Using random assignment of treatments to
experimental units allows for cause-and-effect conclusions between the explanatory and the
response variables because the potential for confounding variables is reduced." (1.13.D.1)

`[EXAM]` An observational study is "a study where treatments are not imposed." (1.10.D.1)
A confounding variable in one "provides an alternative explanation for the observed
relationship … thereby reducing the possibility of concluding a causal relationship", and
"must be associated with both the explanatory variable and the response variable."
(1.10.D.5)

`[EXAM]` The purpose of random assignment is "to create treatment groups that are as similar
as possible with respect to extraneous sources of variation." (1.13.A.7) This is the
justification the article gives for the causation column.

## Flags raised in this session

1. **"Sampling frame" is not framework vocabulary.** I did not find it in the
   essential-knowledge statements for topics 1.10 to 1.13. It is standard in textbooks and
   it names the gap that produces undercoverage, so I kept it, but if you want the article
   to stay strictly inside the framework's word list it should come out.

2. **Convenience sampling is modelled as proximity-weighted, not "the nearest n".** A first
   version took the n students closest to the surveyor, which is deterministic: the
   sampling distribution collapses to a single spike and the article's central picture
   disappears. Weighting selection probability by proximity keeps the bias, gives a genuine
   sampling distribution, and is arguably the more honest model of what a convenience sample
   actually is. Worth knowing that the picture depends on this choice.

3. **Length: 1107 words**, second only to the harmonic series (1128) in the whole corpus.
   The article carries an entire unit's vocabulary, four named biases, four sampling
   methods, and the four-cell table, all of which the brief specifies. The obvious cut is
   the vocabulary section, which is also the part a struggling student most needs. Left long
   deliberately; say the word and I will split it.

4. **The four cells communicated as a table**, so I did not build the second interactive the
   brief offered as a fallback. Four rows of prose would have been worse; the table puts the
   two axes where they can be read against each other.

5. **The population's true mean is 7.100 by luck of the seed**, not by construction — the
   generator produces 6.2 + 1.8x + 0.45z with x uniform, whose expectation is 7.1, and this
   particular draw of 400 landed at 7.09996. Convenient for the prose, and stable because
   the seed is fixed, but not something I engineered.
